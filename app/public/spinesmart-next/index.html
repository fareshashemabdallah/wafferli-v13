Spine-conscious training rules

Reviewed for a history of L5–S1 lumbar disc herniation, cervical disc problems and degenerative disc disease. This is a conservative general plan, not an individualized rehabilitation programme or a guarantee of safety. A physiotherapist should check your symptoms, nerve function, any spinal canal findings, and exercise technique before progressing.

  1. Neutral neck: look straight ahead; do not thrust your head forward or shrug while pushing/pulling.
  2. Supported spine: use the machine backrest/chest pad; keep the lower back stable rather than rounding at the deepest range.
  3. Controlled breathing: exhale during effort; avoid breath-holding or straining.
  4. Easy-to-moderate load: last reps should feel manageable, with 2–4 more reps possible. No max lifts.
  5. Range is individual: stop before your pelvis rolls, neck strains, or symptoms appear.
  6. Allow recovery: take at least a day between strength sessions. A short walk on other days is fine if comfortable.
For now, set aside

The initial programme excludes leg presses, lat pulldowns, loaded cable Pallof presses, bird dogs, heavy deadlifts, heavy barbell squats, weighted spinal flexion/twisting, heavy overhead pressing and movements that cause symptoms. These are initial precautions, not necessarily lifelong restrictions.

Stop and seek help

Stop a movement if it causes new or worsening back/neck pain, shooting pain, numbness, tingling, weakness or clumsiness. Seek urgent assessment for progressive weakness, new hand clumsiness, difficulty balancing or walking, new bladder/bowel problems, or numbness around the saddle/groin region.

Follow-up safety check

After training, check how you feel later that day and the next morning. If symptoms worsen or spread to a limb, do not increase the exercise load. Arrange review with a physiotherapist or doctor.

Why this programme is conservative

Graded activity is encouraged for many people with lumbar and cervical degeneration, but a diagnosis or lack of pain cannot determine safe loading. The initial programme emphasizes level walking and light supported machine movements. Reintroduce harder movements only after individualized review.
